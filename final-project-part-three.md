# Changes made since Part II

After Part II, I used feedback from three friend interviews and my in class critique group to revise the story. I changed the charts first and then updated the text.

| Feedback | Change in Part III |
|---|---|
| Groups appeared in different orders | I sorted the seven groups by organization size and used this order in the charts. |
| The 2022 revenue chart had too much information | I kept the bars, replaced the grey bars with filled areas, and used orange for the 2022 bar of the three groups with their highest year in 2022. |
| One reader said the chart could better show the change from 2019 to the latest year | I colored the chart by direction. Blue means up, orange means down, and grey means about the same. |
| Readers did not understand why one bar went past 100% | I now show each source as a share of income before losses. |

I also added a chart of 2019 total revenue to introduce the size of the seven groups. I added a section on why the recovery paths differ.

## The audience

My main audience is people who attend live performances and may support local arts groups. Most of them do not usually read financial reports. My second audience is people who work or study in the arts.

For these readers, I changed the income structure charts to show income before losses. At first, I showed each source as a share of total revenue. On the IRS tax form, total revenue includes all income lines. Some lines can be negative. For example, a group may lose money on an event or on a sale. This loss makes the total smaller. As a result, the other sources can add up to more than 100%. This was confusing to read. Now I do not subtract these losses, so each bar adds up to 100%.

I also thought about color blind readers. I chose colors in all charts that they can tell apart.

## Final design decisions

**Colors.** One friend wanted bar colors that match the page. I kept the current bar colors because color blind readers can see them clearly.

**2022 revenue chart.** In Part II, I planned to use line charts with light filled areas. In the end, I kept bars and added the filled areas.

**Comparison chart.** In Part II, I planned to test a change of axes. I kept the groups on the x axis and used color to show the direction of change.

**What I learned.** One chart should not carry too much information. It works best with only one or two key points. Otherwise, readers need a lot of time to understand it.

# Final thoughts

Overall, the research and design process went smoothly. The tax filings of each organization are public, and they cover many years. This made my topic manageable.

With more time, I would explore more format options in Tableau. For example, I could not make the chart background transparent, so it is still white. I also just learned how to use Shorthand, so I do not know some advanced design features yet.

My favorite part was trying different chart types in Tableau to find the one that worked best.
